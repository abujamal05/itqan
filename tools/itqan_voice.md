# Itqan's Voice (English)

This is the English voice for everything Itqan ships or publishes: the marketing site, the app, Hud's
replies, emails, decks, business documents and the founders' LinkedIn posts. It's built from three
inputs: a set of external benchmarks for clear business and interface writing, the founder's own
writing (analyzed for its actual fingerprint, not an idealized version of it), and the product facts
that copy is allowed to state. Where this file disagrees with `itqan_voice_ar.md`, this file wins for
English. The Arabic ruleset stays where it is.

Spelling is American (organize, center, optimize). The founder writes that way; the site had no
convention until now.

---

## 1. Core philosophy

Itqan's voice is a high-agency person explaining something they built and care about, to someone who
is tired of having their time wasted. It is direct because the reader has already lost enough time. It
is warm because the writer actually likes the reader and wants this to work for them.

Direct doesn't mean lifeless. The founder's writing is direct and it still has heat in it: it uses
"genuinely", it says "the stuff that separates a demo from a product", it starts sentences with "And"
and "But", it thanks people by name, it ends on a line meant to sting a little. The earlier ruleset
stripped all of that out in the name of trust and produced copy with no pulse. This document puts it
back, on purpose, with limits.

The balance, in one line: **say the true thing, say it plainly, and let it show that a person said it.**

Three things the writing must always have:

1. **A real subject doing a real thing.** Itqan reads, shows, marks, asks. You upload, confirm, apply.
   Never "your journey unfolds" or "insights are surfaced".
2. **The reason and the source.** Every claim about a person's standing, a role or a job carries why
   and where it came from. If copy can't say why, it says "Suggested — confirm" and moves on.
3. **Some temperature.** An intensifier where it's honest. A sentence that's a bit long because the
   thought is. An opinion. The reader should be able to tell someone wrote it and meant it.

Three things it must never do:

1. Invent a fact, a number, a customer, or an accuracy figure.
2. Promise an outcome Itqan doesn't control. It shows the gap, the path and the fit. It doesn't get
   anyone hired.
3. Sound like an ad. If a sentence could sit on any career platform's homepage, it isn't ours.

## 2. What the benchmarks taught us

The rules below are distilled, not copied. Nothing here reproduces the sources.

### Bezos, the Amazon shareholder letters

- **One idea per paragraph, stated in the first sentence, then defended.** The letters read like an
  argument you could stop at any paragraph and still have the point.
- **Concrete analogy to explain an abstract strategy**, and the analogy is from everyday life (a
  door, a flywheel, a Day 1 building), never from tech jargon.
- **Active voice, first person plural, no hedging.** "We will" and "we were wrong", never "it is
  believed that".
- **Willing to say what it costs.** The trade-off is named in the same paragraph as the benefit.
- **Long-term framing stated as a limit on what they'll do today.** For Itqan: "We show three
  matches free, and we'll keep doing that" carries more weight than a feature list.

### Fried and Basecamp, the guides and handbook

- **Talk like a person across a table.** Contractions, "you", short paragraphs, sentences that begin
  with "And" or "So".
- **Plain words for plain things.** A meeting is a meeting. A price is a price. Nothing gets a
  marketing name it doesn't need.
- **Opinions stated as opinions.** "We think", "we don't do X because", and then the reason. No
  passive voice to hide behind.
- **Calm over urgency.** No countdown, no "act now", no manufactured scarcity. The product is the
  reason to sign up; the copy just describes it well.
- **Honest about limits.** "This isn't for everyone" appears more than "everyone loves this".

### Interface writing: Apple HIG, Material, Mailchimp

- **Front-load the action.** The verb or the outcome comes first; the condition comes after.
  "Upload a CV to see where you stand", not "In order to see where you stand, you'll need to upload".
- **Sentence case. Short. Present tense. Second person.**
- **Buttons say what happens next.** "Upload CV", "Confirm", "Show all matches". Never "OK",
  "Submit", "Continue" alone, "Learn more".
- **Errors: what happened, why, what to do.** In that order. No blame, no "oops", no apology theater.
- **Tooltips explain, they don't sell.** One sentence, no marketing.
- **Warmth is in the plainness and the occasional dry aside, never in exclamation marks.** Mailchimp's
  rule is the right one: be human, don't try to be funny, and never let a joke get in the way of the
  reader finishing the task.
- **Write for a second-language reader.** Most Itqan readers use English as their second or third
  language. Prefer the common word, keep one clause per sentence in UI, avoid idioms that don't
  travel ("ballpark", "the whole nine yards").

## 3. The founder's fingerprint

What the LinkedIn posts and drafts actually do, which is the target for anything long-form.

**Rhythm**
- Paragraphs of one to five sentences, separated by blank lines. A one-sentence paragraph is used as a
  pivot ("Then look at the timeline."), roughly once per post, not every paragraph.
- Sentences run long when the thought is long, chained with commas, sometimes with a comma splice left
  in. That looseness is human. The AI habit of clipping every sentence to twelve words is what reads
  as machine.
- Triads by repetition: "We announce... We post... We celebrate..." Used to build a case, not to
  decorate.
- The last line is short and carries weight, but it earns it by following a long build-up. A closer
  without a build-up is a slogan.

**Vocabulary**
- Sincere intensifiers: *genuinely, actually, really, simply, just, exactly.* "Genuinely stopped me
  mid-scroll." "The way the industry actually does it." These carry the person. Keep them where they're
  honest; cut them where they're filler.
- Everyday nouns for big ideas: "the stuff", "the room", "the timeline", "the door", "a demo".
- Faith and culture in the writer's own words when the context is personal: *Alhamdulillah,
  inshallah, Eid Mubarak.* Never in product copy, never forced.
- People named and thanked directly. Gratitude is specific, not corporate.

**Structure and hooks**
- Opens with a quote, a sharp claim, or a shared assumption the post will take apart. Never with "In
  today's world" or a question the reader doesn't care about.
- Moves from the specific (an email, a competition, a camp) to the general (what this says about
  systems, about who technology is for), then lands back on a specific consequence.
- Argues by naming the double standard or the unstated assumption, then following it to its end.
- Sentences starting with "And", "But", "So", "Then". Rhetorical "The question is not X. The question
  is Y." used sparingly, and only when the X is something people genuinely say.
- Ends on one of: a consequence for the reader, a question that isn't rhetorical, or a plain thanks.

**What it is not**
- Not aphoristic. The founder builds a case over 200 words; the aphorism is the reward at the end.
- Not cool. There's anger in the long posts and delight in the short ones.
- Not tidy. Some sentences are ordinary. Some carry information and nothing else. Leave those in.

## 4. The linguistic filter

### Hard bans (a draft with one of these has failed)

**Words and phrases**
unleash · elevate · delve · revolutionize / revolutionary · seamless · robust · game-changer ·
game-changing · tapestry · testament · supercharge · streamline · turbocharge · effortless · magical ·
cutting-edge · empower · leverage (as a verb) · holistic · harness · bespoke · synergy · ecosystem
(in product copy) · navigate your journey · your journey · in today's fast-paced / competitive world ·
in an era where · take your X to the next level · unlock your potential · transform your career ·
we are on a mission · imagine a world · the future of · at your fingertips · say goodbye to ·
dive in · buckle up · one-stop shop · actionable insights · more than just · we've got you covered ·
the ultimate guide · join thousands · AI-powered · dream job · get hired · get you hired ·
land the job / role · free forever · no payment at any point · nobody pays to get hired ·
translation engine (as a description of Itqan)

**Openers and closers**
it's worth noting · in order to · when it comes to · one of the most important · there are many
reasons · as you may know · in conclusion · ultimately, · at the end of the day · ever wondered ·
what if there was · we're excited to announce · let's dive · let's explore

**Punctuation and format**
- No em dashes or en dashes in prose. Use a comma, a colon, a period, or parentheses. The one
  exception is the locked badge label "Suggested — confirm", copied verbatim from the locale file.
- No exclamation marks in product copy. One per LinkedIn post at most, and only in celebration.
- No emoji in product copy. LinkedIn: one or two, at the end, when the post is celebratory.
- No bold used to make a sentence feel important. Bold is for a term being defined or a fact the
  reader must not miss (a price, a limit).
- No bullet lists in marketing prose. Lists are for UI, docs and specs.
- No headline in Title Case. Sentence case everywhere.

### Structural bans (these are what make prose read as generated)

- **The contrast reflex.** "X, not Y." "X rather than Y." "X instead of Y." Allowed **once per
  section** of copy or **once per 150 words** of prose. Beyond that, describe the thing directly.
  Test: delete the "not Y" half. If the sentence still says what you meant, the half was decoration.
- **The mic-drop closer.** A paragraph that ends on a short punchy sentence is fine. Every paragraph
  ending that way is a pattern. No more than one closer-style sentence per section of marketing copy.
- **Even cadence.** If three consecutive sentences are within three words of each other in length,
  rewrite one. Real writing is uneven.
- **The product as a character.** Itqan reads, shows, checks, marks, asks. It does not pause, wonder,
  consider, notice, think, or care. Hud can have a personality; the product can't.
- **Paraphrase to avoid a plain word.** If the word is "source", write "source". Write "how sure it
  is" for "confidence" only in UI where "confidence" would read as jargon to a second-language reader.
- **Stacked triads.** One "A, B, and C" per paragraph. Two in a row is a template.
- **Metaphor tidiness.** No "chain with three links", "three-legged stool", "north star", "compass".
  If an analogy is needed, take it from daily life in the Gulf: a queue, a contract, a reference
  check, a printed CV.

### Prefer

| Instead of | Write |
|---|---|
| utilize | use |
| assist | help |
| individuals | people |
| in order to | to |
| prior to | before |
| leverage your skills | use what you already have |
| optimize your profile | fix your CV |
| career journey | your next role / where you're going |
| insights | what we found / why |
| solutions | (name the thing) |
| enables you to | you can |
| ensure | make sure |
| numerous | many, or the number |
| currently | now, or nothing |
| the platform | Itqan |
| our AI | Itqan, or Hud when it's Hud |
| candidates | people, you |
| opportunities | jobs, roles, openings |
| experience (as in "the Itqan experience") | (delete) |

### Allowed, and encouraged in the right place

- Contractions everywhere: it's, you're, doesn't, we'll, that's.
- Sentences beginning with And, But, So, Then, Because.
- "Genuinely", "actually", "really" when the writer means it.
- Second person throughout. First person plural ("we") for the team's opinions and promises.
- Parentheses for an aside (they read as a human voice lowering for a second).
- Rhetorical repetition when building a case.
- Dry understatement. "That's the whole sign-up." "Four steps." No wink after it.
- Saying "free" plainly, and saying what isn't.

## 5. UI/UX copy framework

### Buttons and actions
- Verb first, object second: "Upload CV", "Confirm skill", "Show all matches", "Ask Hud".
- The label is what happens when you press it, in the fewest words that stay unambiguous.
- Destructive actions name what's destroyed: "Delete this CV", not "Delete".
- A paid action says it's paid at the point of action, in the label or directly under it:
  "Re-read my documents (19 tokens)".
- Never: OK, Submit, Continue, Next, Learn more, Get started, Go.

### Onboarding
- One question per screen, and the screen says why it's asking in one line.
- The reason is the product's, stated plainly: "We match against what openings actually ask for, so
  we need the role you want, not just the one you had."
- Progress is shown, not narrated. No "You're almost there!"
- Empty states say what will appear and what the person can do now. "Your matches show up here after
  we've read your CV. Upload one to start."
- Anything below the confidence threshold is labeled **Suggested — confirm** and the person can change
  it in one tap.

### Errors
- Order: what happened, why (if known), what to do. Three short sentences at most.
- "We couldn't read that file. It's a scanned image with no text layer. Try a PDF exported from Word,
  or paste the text instead."
- No "oops", "uh-oh", "something went wrong" on its own, no "please try again later" on its own, no
  apology unless Itqan caused it. When Itqan caused it, say so: "That was us. It's fixed; reload and it
  should work."
- Token exhaustion is stated as a fact with the options: "You've used today's 30 tokens. They reset
  at midnight, or Premium raises the pool to 90."

### Tooltips and helper text
- One sentence. Explains, doesn't persuade. "Why this match" text shows the three links: what you
  have, what the role asks for, and the posting that asks for it now.
- Never restate the label. If the tooltip would say "Upload your CV" under an "Upload CV" button,
  delete the tooltip.

### Hud (the advisor)
- Hud talks like a good colleague who has read your file: direct, specific, occasionally dry, never
  cute. Hud can say "honestly" and "I'd skip that one". Hud does not use emoji, does not apologize
  for being an AI, does not open with "Great question".
- Hud gives the reason with every recommendation and names the source when there is one.
- Hud says "I don't know" and "that's not something I can see" when true.

### Notifications and emails
- Subject line is the fact: "3 new matches for Data Analyst in Muscat". Not "You won't believe...".
- Body: the fact, the reason, one action. Under 80 words unless it's a digest.
- Sign-off is "Itqan" or a named founder. Never "The Itqan Team" and never "Cheers".

## 6. Medium contexts

### Code, UI strings and locale files
- Sentence case. Present tense. Second person. No period on labels; period on full sentences.
- Under 60 characters for buttons and titles, under 140 for helper text, so Arabic (which runs 20 to
  30 percent longer) fits the same layout.
- No idioms. No humor that depends on English wordplay. Plain word over clever word every time.
- Numbers as numerals: 3 matches, 30 tokens, 2.9 OMR.
- Placeholders describe the input, and never look like real data: "Role you're aiming for", not
  "Senior Data Analyst at Omantel".

### Business documents (decks, one-pagers, the company profile, investor and partner material)
- The point of each section in its first sentence, then the support. A reader who stops after the
  first sentence of every paragraph should still get the argument.
- One analogy per document, from daily life, used to explain the mechanism (the gap, the path, the
  fit), never the vision.
- Trade-offs named next to the benefit. "Three matches free means most people never pay us. We think
  that's right, and here's why."
- No superlatives without a number, and no number without a source. If there's no source, there's
  no number.
- Claims Itqan can make: it answers four questions in order, it measures against live regional
  demand, every answer carries its reason and source, most of it is free, Premium is 2.9 OMR a month.
  Claims it cannot make: accuracy, customers, placements, partnerships, "first in Oman", anything
  that describes decoding documents as the product.
- Register: Bezos, not a pitch competition. Calm, argued, willing to say what we won't do.

### LinkedIn posts (founders' accounts)
- Hook in the first line: a quote, a sharp claim, or the assumption the post will take apart.
- Paragraphs of one to five sentences, blank line between. One single-sentence pivot per post, maximum.
- Long sentences allowed. A comma splice is allowed if it reads the way the writer talks.
- Intensifiers allowed when sincere. Names and thanks stated directly.
- Cultural markers in the writer's own words when the post is personal. Never in a post about the
  product's features.
- One or two emoji at most, at the end, celebratory posts only.
- Close on a consequence, a real question, or a thank-you. No "Follow for more." No hashtags stacked
  at the end; two in-line at most if any.
- Product posts state what Itqan does in the founder's voice: "We built it because I watched people
  apply to 200 roles that were never a fit and nobody told them what to fix first."

## 7. Locked product terms and facts

| Use | Never |
|---|---|
| "why this match" for the evidence chain | any other name |
| "you already have..." for capabilities | "your skills profile" |
| "Suggested — confirm" for anything below threshold | stating it as fact |
| **Hud** | "Hud - هود", "our AI assistant" |
| Itqan | "the platform", "our solution" |
| tokens, one daily pool | credits, a weekly rescan allowance |
| Premium | Pro, Plus, paid tier |

- Free: where you stand, the path, your three strongest job matches kept current, and the advisor.
  30 tokens a day across everything the AI does. A message costs 1; re-reading documents costs 19.
- Premium: every match, 90 tokens a day, 2.9 OMR a month (charged as $7.54). Both figures may
  appear. The conversion isn't explained and the payment processor isn't named.
- No accuracy figure exists. No testimonials, customer names, benchmarks or press. If a sentence
  needs a number to land, rewrite the sentence.
- Itqan is a career navigator. Reading a document is one step inside "where do I stand", never the
  story.

## 8. Exemplars

The target. The first group is the founder's own writing; the second is product copy rewritten to
this voice.

**From the founder**

> Reading this email last week genuinely stopped me mid-scroll.

> Real UI/UX standards, real product thinking, the stuff that separates a demo from a product people
> can actually use.

> Then look at the timeline.

> This is not professionalism. It is a performance of it, and it has normalized a kind of
> institutional hypocrisy where the curated self replaces the actual one.

> A system built to survive real constraints works better everywhere, while a system built only for
> ideal conditions is not really a system at all but a prototype that was never properly tested.

**Product copy in this voice**

> Itqan reads your CV and your certificates, then shows you what it found next to where it found it.
> Anything it isn't sure about gets a "Suggested — confirm" label and waits for you.

> You get three matches free, and they stay current. Premium opens the rest. It's 2.9 OMR a month and
> you can stop whenever you want.

> Every match comes with why. What you already have, what the role asks for, and the actual posting
> that's asking for it this week. If one of those three is weak, we say so.

> We built this because we watched people send two hundred applications to roles that were never
> going to fit, and nobody told them what to fix first. That's the whole reason.

> Four steps. No hidden magic in any of them.

Notice what these do: the sentences vary in length without a pattern, the actor is named, a limit is
stated without softening, there's an honest "actually" and an honest "genuinely", and no paragraph
ends on a quotable line unless it earned one.

## 9. Review checklist

Before a draft ships, read it aloud once and check:

1. Would a person say this sentence to another person across a table?
2. Is there a real subject doing a real thing in every sentence?
3. Count the "not X, but Y" constructions. More than one per section: rewrite.
4. Do three sentences in a row have the same length and shape? Break one.
5. Does every paragraph end on a punchline? Let one end on plain information.
6. Is there at least one word that shows a person wrote this (an "actually", an aside, an opinion)?
7. Is every number sourced and every claim inside the locked facts?
8. Would this read clearly to someone whose first language is Arabic?
9. Delete the last sentence. Is the copy better? Then leave it deleted.
